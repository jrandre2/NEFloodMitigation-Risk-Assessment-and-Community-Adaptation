## Abstract

Regulatory flood maps identify where hazard is located but leave a parallel governance question under-specified: who holds that mapped exposure. This study addresses that measurement gap by linking parcel-level regulatory flood exposure to a decision-relevant ownership typology. Using all single-family residential parcels in Douglas County, Nebraska, we classify each owner-of-record into five organizational forms—Individual, LLC, Corporation, Trust, and Government/Nonprofit—and measure local portfolio footprint using a fine-tuned BERT transformer. We then estimate conditional associations between ownership structure and SFHA location using a modified Poisson regression with neighborhood fixed effects. After adjusting for parcel value, parcel size, and micro-location, single-parcel LLCs exhibit the highest prevalence of regulatory flood exposure—approximately 67% higher than single-parcel Individuals. Elevated exposure is also observed for multi-parcel LLCs and multi-parcel Trusts. Within-form contrasts reveal that single-parcel LLCs are more exposed than their multi-parcel counterparts, consistent with liability-siloing strategies. This reproducible framework makes the ownership dimension of floodplain exposure empirically visible, supporting portfolio-aware administration and more precise urban flood governance.
